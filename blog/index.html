I’m writing this on the walk back from class

I think I’m a pretty logical person. I ground my life and decisions on logical principles like expected value.

I cut everything in my life that wouldn’t bring me to my goal: Get rich as quickly as possible to buy C8 Z06 (and any car that I want in the future) and make a lasting contribution to humanity.

I bought a bike to save time commuting between my class and dorm. I took the bus when the bike got stolen. I skipped a shower if it meant studying or working on my startup or research or projects for longer. I skipped the gym and faded going out some weekends.

I told myself anything that wasn’t studying, or working on my startups or research was a waste of time. By fading everything else, I was being as optimal as possible.

My roommate, Bhargav, is the complete opposite of me: he takes long showers, sleeps early even if it means less studying or doing less work; Goes to the gym right before a test; and plays IM soccer even though he sucks at soccer. He solely walks to class. He'll walk 30-40 minutes to a study spot each way.

Last night, I was having a conversation with him about how I didn't have time to go to the gym: all my free time was going into studying or startup. He said that was the dumbest thing I had ever said. He told me I do have time. My schedule was the issue. My mindset was wrong.

The mind needs time to think, it needs physical health to be optimal. Starving it of that, was making me less optimal. I made up for that lack of break by doomscrolling which made me sad, regretful, and reduced my motivation. For the longest time, I thought I had no discipline, no focus. I thought I had to drink a monster to lock-in or needed to spend longer studying because I was getting bad grades. My mind and my body needed time to switch gears and a break in order to be optimal.

Today, I’m writing this as I walk back from class. It’s is the first time since 2023 I have written something this long, and this meaningful, without the use of outside assistance (forgive any grammar mistakes). And I did it on my phone on the default notes app.

I’m about to reach my room now, so I’m concluding this blog with this last thought.

It's so easy to get swept up by the excitement that is AI right now. I fell for that trap, I might still be stuck in it. Squinting into the future, constantly building. I need to pause, reflect on the past at the same time. I can't learn and get better just by doing more, I have to learn from my mistakes and think about how I spend my time. I'm grateful to be surrounded by people like Bhargav who are different than me.
