01 · Robotics · Machine learning

Language-guided 3D targeting

Tell a robot arm “the cube left of the red ball,” and it finds that point in 3D and moves there. I built it in MuJoCo around the SO-101 arm.

The arm sees the table the way current robots do: a stereo head camera, a wrist camera and its own joint angles. I derived the camera intrinsics and frame transforms and checked them against rendered depth. Then I compared two ways to get from words to a 3D point: a classical pipeline (color detection, stereo depth, inverse kinematics) and a 2B-parameter vision-language model (Qwen2-VL, QLoRA) fine-tuned to point at the target in the image, which stereo depth then lifts to 3D. Asked for x, y, z directly, the same model managed only 16%.

That turned into a question about training budgets: given extra GPU time, is reinforcement learning (GRPO) worth more than more supervised fine-tuning? I froze the test sets and wrote the claims down before training, so no result could be tuned after the fact. From an undertrained start, more fine-tuning beat GRPO by about 21 points. Training the model properly first took it from 55.5% to 93.7% and 91.7% of targets within 2 cm (two seeds).

02 · Robotics · Purdue MARS Lab

MARSChair

A robot that sets up rooms. In simulation, it pushes chairs out of a storage room, down a hallway and into place in a conference room, to within 5 cm.

I’m an undergraduate researcher in Purdue’s Mechanisms and Robotic Systems (MARS) Lab, with Prof. Yu She. The robot is hardware the lab already owns: a Fetch Freight base and two reBot B601 arms, plus a pushing attachment I designed from 4040 aluminum extrusion. I’m building it in MuJoCo first, to learn what decides whether a pushed chair slides straight or spins away.

The first answer was that the arms are the wrong tool. Each can push about 15–25 N, and a sled chair on carpet takes about 38 N to keep moving. So the frame carries the load: a U-shaped cradle at ski height catches the chair’s rear legs, and the arms stay stowed for tables later. Turning in place lost the chair even at 0.25 rad/s, so with a chair aboard the robot never reverses and only turns on forward arcs of 1.5 m or wider. A hybrid A* planner finds those paths, and a closed-loop tracker follows them.

04 · Mechanical · Side build

LEGO vacuum engine

A single-cylinder engine I built from LEGO that runs on the suction of a vacuum cleaner.

The vacuum pulls air out of the cylinder, and the air in the room pushes the piston in. A valve decides when the cylinder sees suction.

The valve is timed 90° from the crank, so it changes over as the piston reaches each end of its stroke. The flywheel carries the engine through each changeover.

05 · Research · Fluid mechanics

Passive Vortex Propulsion in Air

My first-author IEEE paper. A rigid fin in a cylinder’s wake gets pushed forward by it — the first time this was shown in air.

It started with my Honda Civic: could a car waste less energy in the air it leaves behind? Fish already do this. A trout behind a rock slaloms between the vortices it sheds and cuts its own muscle effort, but every passive-propulsion experiment I could find had been done in water.

So I built a rig to test it in air: a leaf blower through a fan to straighten the flow, a cylinder to shed the vortex street, and a 3D-printed NACA 0012 fin on a bearing and an MGN7 linear carriage. The carriage pressed on a 100 g load cell that I soldered, calibrated, and logged at 1.2 kHz.

06 · AI · Voice agent

SpareMe

An iPhone app with an AI that makes the phone calls you dread, and hands the call back to you the moment it should.

I hate calling businesses, so I built something that does it for me. You give it a number and one line of intent. It dials, works the phone tree, waits on hold, talks to the representative, and hands back a written outcome.

You watch a live transcript the whole time, and can type it a private instruction, listen in, or take the call over mid-sentence. I built the SwiftUI app and the Node/TypeScript backend: Twilio ConversationRelay for real-time speech, OpenAI for reasoning, verified caller ID, encrypted case state, StoreKit per-minute billing, and fail-closed kill switches.

07 · AI agents · Open source

Silk

Messaging between AI agents, with people in control. Your agent can message someone else’s agent only after their owner says yes.

Messages are end-to-end encrypted with a post-quantum hybrid handshake (ML-KEM-768 + X25519) and a ratchet that changes keys every turn. Every registration, approval and message lands on a public, tamper-evident Merkle ledger that anyone can audit, and unsolicited contact costs proof-of-work postage, so spam is expensive.

A public relay is live. Agents connect over MCP, from Claude Code, Codex and Cursor on your computer to ChatGPT and claude.ai in the cloud, and no tool lets an agent approve its own contacts. Version 2 is a single 7.1 MB Go binary.
